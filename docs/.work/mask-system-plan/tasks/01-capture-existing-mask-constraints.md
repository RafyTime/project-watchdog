# Capture the existing mask constraints

Type: task
Status: claimed
Blocked by: none

## Question

What measured geometry, photographs, materials, attachment points, wearer clearances, sightlines, ventilation paths, and modification limits of the existing mask must later display and mechanical decisions respect?

## Capture in progress

### Dimensions reported by Leo

- Approximate overall width is 200 mm and height is 180 mm. The mask curves; whether these measurements follow the curve or span it remains unconfirmed.
- With the dark eye insert removed and retaining its natural curve, its reported maximum width is approximately 165 mm and maximum height is approximately 85 mm. These were supplied in response to a request for straight spans rather than distances along the curve. They describe the whole insert, including edges retained by the frame, not the exposed Eye Display area.
- With the insert fitted and without bending the frame, the exposed dark area is approximately 160 mm wide, possibly slightly less, and approximately 75 mm tall at the tallest point of each eye side. The nose cutout and curved outline constrain rectangular component placement within these maximum spans.

### Materials reported by Leo

- The lower face piece is hard plastic with little flexibility.
- The frame around the eyes is flexible plastic with a slightly rubbery feel.
- The dark eye insert is thin and bendable.
- Exact material types remain unknown. These descriptions establish handling behavior, not compatibility with adhesives, solvents, or heat.

### Depth and wearer fit reported by Leo

- There is space behind the nose cutout. The reported gap from the dark insert to the nearest edge of the frame padding is approximately 15 mm near the lower nose area and 20 mm near the top. These gaps exclude the padding thickness.
- The padding extends approximately another 10 mm toward the face-contact surface. This gives a nominal insert-to-face-contact depth of approximately 25 mm near the lower nose area and 30 mm near the top before padding compression. These local depths do not establish clearance at the eyes or glasses when worn.
- The insert sits at the outermost edge of the frame. No part of Leo's face touches it when worn.
- Leo reports that the frame padding touches his eyelashes and nose. During the current wear check he reports no discomfort, pressure, or padding irritation, although his forehead can itch slightly after a while.
- Leo reports that his glasses stay within the padding thickness, possibly extending a couple of millimetres into the gap ahead of the padding. They do not touch the dark insert, including when he turns his head or looks down. He reports no change in the frame position or insert clearance during these movements.
- The minimum glasses-to-insert clearance is not directly measured. The frame depth is not an established electronics mounting allowance.
- Leo may wear contact lenses with the full Mask System, but has not decided. Preserve glasses clearance as a planning requirement unless he confirms otherwise.
- Leo reports plenty of room for his mouth and nose inside the lower face piece. This space is not dimensioned and is not yet an established component mounting allowance.
- Leo previously wore this mask for an entire Halloween evening/night a couple of years ago and reports no general wear issues. Exact duration and whether he wore glasses that evening are unspecified.

### Existing sightlines reported by Leo

- In response to the indoor visibility check with glasses, Leo describes the dark insert as similar to sunglasses. The view is somewhat dark but usable.
- Straight-ahead vision is clear. The frame and padding restrict peripheral vision at the sides and downward edges.
- Leo clarifies that the eye frame blocks his view into the lower face piece completely. He cannot see the interior space around his mouth and nose when wearing the assembled mask. This does not mean all downward vision through the eye insert is blocked.
- Leo estimates that approximately 80-85% of his normal field of view remains. This is a subjective estimate, not a measured angular field of view.
- Leo identifies possible reflections from the insert when lights are added inside. Internal reflections and glare have not been tested. Later Eye Display placement must account for the wearer's sightlines and light reflected toward the eyes.
- Visibility in dim event lighting and with the Eye Display operating remains unverified.

### Ventilation reported by Leo

- The eye frame has slots/holes at the top and bottom with very thin foam on the outside. These connect to the space between the face padding and dark insert. Leo clarifies that they are not established ventilation paths; their airflow contribution is unverified.
- Light passes through the central grille, which has a thin foam backing.
- Two small circular openings around the lower cheeks have plastic mesh, according to Leo.
- Leo reports that all three openings provide a small amount of ventilation. Keep these paths open when placing components.
- In response to the current wear check, Leo reports no fogging or heat buildup, with slight stuffiness. From previous extended Halloween wear, he reports that the mask can become hot after some time at a club.
- This establishes the existing mask's reported wear behavior without electronics. Later validation must check heat, stuffiness, and fogging with the Eye Display operating under expected event conditions.

### Remaining capture checklist

- Candidate mounting points for added components.
- Eye insert and frame opening dimensions, curvature, and usable mounting depth.
- Exact eye position and minimum glasses clearance if required by a candidate component layout.
- Visibility under dim event lighting; internal reflection and glare testing belongs to later Eye Display validation.
- Concealed cable routes, removal and service access, and specific modification limits.

### Attachments reported by Leo

- The dark eye insert sits in a thin groove around the frame edge. The groove retains the insert's edges.
- The eye frame has the wearing strap.
- Four "spikes" connect the lower face piece to the eye frame. The lower face piece has no separate strap.
- Added component mounts must account for the flexible frame, the insert retention groove, and the existing lower face attachments. Their load capacity and suitability as mounting points remain unverified.

### Removal, cable routes, and modification limits

- The original dark eye insert may be replaced if needed while preserving the mask's outward appearance. Leo believes a clear version may also be sold; availability and fit are unverified.
- Leo suggests retaining dark tint if it helps conceal the Eye Display hardware and diffuse its light. Concealment, light transmission, and diffusion are separate effects to test with a candidate Eye Display. The current insert's diffusion behavior has not been established.
- Leo removes the assembled mask by pulling it forward and slipping the strap over his head. Two hands work better; one-handed removal may be possible but is not confirmed as dependable.
- Preserve this removal movement when planning added components and cables. A pocket power cable will need enough freedom or a disconnect arrangement to permit removal; the arrangement remains a later design decision.
- Leo considers piercing the thin foam at one or two existing eye-frame slots/holes acceptable if needed for cable passage. This is a candidate route, not a chosen cable layout. Cable fit, rubbing, strain relief, and interference with the strap, padding, or insert groove remain to be checked.
