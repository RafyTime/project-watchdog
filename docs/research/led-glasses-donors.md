### Teardown and the one-week build

The user supplied [the 2017 CHEMION integration video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HKVc8a4pHy4) and [the 2018 GreekGadgetGuru video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=td1MxwrMHIo). The parent agent's inspection of the first video at approximately 6:21 observed soldering external wires onto the front PCB. This is relevant evidence that donor integration may require electrical relocation, not merely removing arms. It does not establish the construction of the 2024 COLOR version or Lixada/Queen.Y/Gugxiom hardware. The second video requires separate inspection before its construction claims can be used.

Keep a donor's controller, battery and original wiring intact for first light. Verify a static custom X and reboot behavior before modifying it. If the battery or control button is in an arm that obstructs the mask, moving it is an additional wiring and mounting task. Do not count a donor as the faster architecture until that layout is known. No source found here permits treating these fronts as flexible or splitting them into independent eye boards.

For the current comparison, prefer Lixada and CHEMION for exact-listing inspection, followed by Queen.Y if its selectable color and dimensions work. The inexpensive four-color Gugxiom has insufficient evidence of custom drawing. A second option should address a different failure mode and must also satisfy the new minimum Expression height; buying a known-too-small module pair would not provide that backup.

## Earlier finding, 2026-10-23

Ready-made glasses can remove firmware and display-wiring work if their existing electronics stay together. They do not remove the mask-fit problem. No donor examined has verified removable independent eye boards, flexible display construction, pocket-controlled restart, or automatic startup with a saved X within ten seconds. Treat these as inexpensive candidates for inspection, not proven substitutes for documented development boards.

The best inexpensive white-light lead is the DIGI/Magic family at leds-blink.de. The best documented firmware is CHEMION MONO, but sourcing and its price weaken it under the two-option budget. The lowest-risk donor integration would retain the front, controller, wiring and battery together, removing only clearly mechanical parts after inspection. This is an engineering recommendation, not a documented disassembly procedure.

## Integration assessment

The mask's bow and unmeasured glasses clearance make a rigid full-width donor uncertain even when its nominal width fits. Separate development modules preserve more placement freedom. Ready-made glasses win on first light and drawing tools; documented modules win on known power behavior and startup firmware. That tradeoff favors one donor plus a technically different module pair if the final basket fits EUR 100, rather than two undocumented donor variants.

Before destructive disassembly, run a brief unmodified check: draw X eyes, close the Phone Controller, power off and on, and confirm brightness and static retention. Check the intact front against the mask outline. If either fails, avoid spending the remaining build time reverse-engineering the device. A successful internal-battery run still does not satisfy the agreed pocket restart criterion; retain that discrepancy explicitly unless Leo changes the operating requirement.

No donor currently passes every [runtime, restart and wearability criterion](../.work/mask-system-plan/tasks/04-define-power-reliability-and-safety-criteria.md) on published evidence. In particular, no source established sweat protection, four hours at the chosen brightness behind this insert, usable dim-light navigation with prescription glasses, or safe mounting clearance. Those are assembly acceptance checks, not facts a party-glasses listing can establish.
